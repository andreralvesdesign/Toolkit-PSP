Before you start

An initial response of human support

Not every risk situation requires Psychological First Aid. However, when there is acute emotional distress, dysregulation or significant impact, Psychological First Aid can work as an initial response of human support and containment.

    The essentials

    What Psychological First Aid is

    Click on each of the four topics to open the information. 0 of 4 seen

    What is it?

    • A set of simple, human and structured responses
    • Intended to support people in acute emotional distress
    • Applicable straight away, after difficult or critical situations

    When is it useful?

    • After unexpected or difficult news
    • In situations of visible emotional crisis
    • After accidents, intense conflicts or disturbing events
    • When someone shows clear signs of emotional dysregulation

    Why it matters

    • It reduces the immediate impact of stress
    • It helps restore a sense of safety
    • It reduces emotional isolation
    • It makes it easier to access additional support, if needed

    What it involves

    • Being present in a calm and respectful way
    • Listening carefully and without judgement
    • Showing empathy and availability
    • Respecting the other person’s pace and limits

    The essentials

    What it is NOT

    • It is not psychotherapy
    • It does not require specialised clinical training
    • It does not mean solving the person’s problem
    • It does not replace professional psychological support

    How to act

    The 3 golden rules

    Three simple principles that define the ideal stance when accompanying and supporting someone. In each one, what comes first is the attitude to favour over the most common impulse.

    • RULE 1

      Presence> before solution

      Be present before solving.

    • RULE 2

      Listening> before advice

      Listen before advising.

    • RULE 3

      Support> before control

      Accompany without deciding for the person.

    In practice

    When and who can apply Psychological First Aid

    When to apply it

    1. 1As early as possible after a difficult or critical situation
    2. 2When there are signs of emotional distress (e.g. anxiety, crying, confusion)
    3. 3In events such as conflicts, serious mistakes, accidents or difficult news
    4. 4Whenever the person seems overwhelmed or dysregulated

    Who can apply it

    All employees and leaders, whatever their role or area. No clinical experience is required.

    How to apply it

    Observe, Listen, Link

    Click on each of the three steps to open the information.

    This means looking for:

    • information about what happened and what is happening
    • who needs help
    • safety risks
    • physical injuries
    • basic and immediate practical needs
    • emotional reactions

    This refers to how the person helping:

    • approaches the person
    • introduces themselves
    • pays attention and listens actively
    • accepts the other person’s feelings
    • helps to calm the person in distress
    • asks about needs and concerns
    • helps find solutions to immediate needs and problems

    This means helping the person to:

    • access information
    • get in touch with family and their support network
    • deal with practical problems
    • access services and other support
