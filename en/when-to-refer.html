When to refer

When informal support is no longer enough

Referral happens when informal support, such as Psychological First Aid, is no longer enough to meet the person’s needs. In these cases, it becomes important to involve specialist resources, ensuring continuity of care, protection and appropriate follow-up.

Referral should be considered whenever:

  • 1Emotional distress is intense, persistent or gets worse over time
  • 2The person has difficulty recovering with informal support alone
  • 3There are signs of risk to themselves or to other people
  • 4The needs go beyond the scope of Psychological First Aid
  • 5There is a significant impact on daily, personal, social or professional functioning

What to look for when deciding to refer

Signs of risk

Signs of risk are indicators of significant psychological distress and greater emotional vulnerability. They may arise from changes in the person’s behaviour, emotions or ability to function.

Some examples include

  • Marked changes in behaviour (isolation, agitation, apathy)
  • Expressions of hopelessness, emptiness or a lack of meaning
  • A marked decline in personal, social or professional functioning
  • Increased use of alcohol or other substances
  • Difficulty coping with emotions or everyday situations
  • Very intense emotional reactions, or reactions out of proportion to the situation

Functional incapacity

Functional incapacity refers to the person’s difficulty or inability to carry out everyday tasks and maintain their usual functioning.

It can show up as

  • Difficulty carrying out basic tasks (self-care, work, study or daily responsibilities)
  • Inability to make simple decisions or solve everyday problems
  • Frequent absences or a significant drop in performance
  • A feeling of being “stuck”, unable to move forward or act
  • Difficulty maintaining social relationships or usual responsibilities

Persistent symptoms

Persistent symptoms are emotional, cognitive or physical reactions that continue over time without significant improvement, interfering with the person’s wellbeing.

They may include

  • Persistent anxiety, sadness or irritability
  • Sleep problems (insomnia, broken sleep, nightmares)
  • Intrusive thoughts or difficulty “switching off”
  • Constant emotional tiredness or a feeling of exhaustion
  • Difficulty concentrating or a feeling of mental overload
  • Physical symptoms associated with stress (tension, pain, fatigue)

The purpose of referral

It is not to replace initial support, but to ensure that the person receives the type of help best suited to their needs.
